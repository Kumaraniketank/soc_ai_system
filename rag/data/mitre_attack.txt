MITRE ATT&CK Tactics:

Brute Force Attack:
Repeated failed login attempts targeting accounts.

Ransomware:
Malware encrypts files and demands payment.

PowerShell Attack:
Suspicious encoded PowerShell commands may indicate malware execution.

Credential Dumping:
Attackers attempt to steal credentials from memory.

Privilege Escalation:
Attackers gain higher-level permissions.

Reconnaissance:
Adversaries gather information about the target before launching an attack.

Resource Development:
Attackers acquire infrastructure, tools, or accounts to support operations.

Initial Access:
Adversaries gain a foothold into the network via phishing, exploits, or stolen credentials.

Execution:
Malicious code is run on a target system to carry out attacker objectives.

Persistence:
Attackers maintain access across reboots, credential changes, or interruptions.

Privilege Escalation:
Attackers gain higher-level permissions to access sensitive systems or data.

Defense Evasion:
Adversaries hide their presence by disabling security tools or obfuscating activity.

Credential Access:
Attackers steal usernames and passwords from memory, files, or network traffic.

Discovery:
Adversaries map the environment to understand systems, users, and network layout.

Lateral Movement:
Attackers move through the network to reach high-value targets.

Collection:
Sensitive data is gathered from endpoints, emails, or shared drives prior to exfiltration.

Command and Control:
Attackers communicate with compromised systems to issue commands remotely.

Exfiltration:
Stolen data is transferred out of the environment to attacker-controlled infrastructure.

Impact:
Adversaries disrupt, destroy, or manipulate systems to achieve their final objective.

CVE Severity Levels:

Critical (9.0–10.0):
Remotely exploitable vulnerabilities requiring no authentication or user interaction.

High (7.0–8.9):
Serious flaws that can lead to full system compromise with minimal conditions.

Medium (4.0–6.9):
Vulnerabilities requiring local access or specific conditions to exploit.

Low (0.1–3.9):
Limited-impact flaws with significant exploitation barriers.

Known Exploited Vulnerability (KEV):
A CVE confirmed by CISA to be actively exploited in real-world attacks.

Malware Types:

Ransomware:
Malware encrypts files and demands payment for the decryption key.

Infostealer:
Silently harvests credentials, browser data, and session tokens from infected machines.

RAT (Remote Access Trojan):
Gives attackers full remote control over a compromised endpoint.

Loader/Dropper:
Delivers and installs additional malware payloads onto a victim system.

Wiper:
Destructive malware designed to permanently destroy data with no recovery option.

Botnet:
Network of infected machines controlled remotely for spam, DDoS, or fraud.

Rootkit:
Hides malware and attacker activity deep within the operating system.

Keylogger:
Records every keystroke to capture passwords and sensitive user input.

SOC Playbook Types:

Phishing Response:
Analyze, contain, and purge malicious emails while notifying affected users.

Ransomware Response:
Isolate hosts, preserve forensic evidence, assess backups, and engage IR team.

Account Compromise:
Reset credentials, revoke active sessions, audit logs, and enforce MFA.

Malware Infection:
Isolate the endpoint, extract IOCs, sweep for lateral spread, and reimage.

Brute Force Attack:
Repeated failed login attempts targeting accounts to gain unauthorized access.

Credential Dumping:
Attackers extract credentials from memory, registries, or local credential stores.

PowerShell Attack:
Suspicious encoded PowerShell commands may indicate fileless malware execution.

Insider Threat:
Place legal hold, revoke access, and preserve forensic evidence for investigation.

DDoS Attack:
Engage upstream filtering, apply rate limits, and coordinate with ISP for mitigation.

Privilege Escalation:
Attackers exploit misconfigurations or vulnerabilities to gain elevated system access.
